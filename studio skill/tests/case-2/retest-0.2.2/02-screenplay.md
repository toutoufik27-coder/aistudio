# The Dry Well

## Logline

In the third year of a drought, a well-digger's widow must finish her dead husband's distrusted shaft alone, against her sister-in-law's council, before the village spends its only money on the wrong hole.

## Synopsis

The village well gives up its last grit. RUTH, head of the council, collects the village's money for one attempt: deepening the old well. ILSE, widow of the well-digger Anders, leaves the queue without paying. Behind her house stands his unfinished shaft, eleven metres deep and dry, and in her pocket his notebook, which says fourteen. Ruth comes for Anders's windlass. She once read one of her brother's numbers aloud to the council, and that shaft collapsed. Ilse refuses to hand the windlass over, and Ruth withdraws the council's timber. Ilse digs alone, but below the last of Anders's shoring the bare wall begins to slide. With no timber and no money, she pulls down Anders's tool shed plank by plank and lines the shaft with it. At the old well the council's money runs out on dry rock. At fourteen metres Ilse's floor is dry too; one spade deeper, water comes. In the morning the village queues in her yard, across the pale ground where the shed stood. Ruth waits at the gate with the others, takes her water, and leaves by the gate. Anders was right. The shed is gone, and Ruth no longer comes through the kitchen.

## Structure note

A single escalating situation, seven scenes, no subplot. Three minutes cannot carry acts; one decision pushed through three rising prices (the council's help, the shed, Ruth) can. Each scene is caused by the one before it.

Estimated runtime: 3:05 against a 3:00 target (S01 25 s, S02 35 s, S03 25 s, S04 20 s, S05 20 s, S06 30 s, S07 30 s). Dialogue is 68 words, about 27 seconds at 150 words per minute; the rest is action timed by beat.

## Character and world facts

All fiction. Items marked (concept) come from 01-concept.md; items marked (new) are invented for this screenplay.

### Characters

- **C01 ILSE**, 60s, widow of the village well-digger (concept). Wants to finish the shaft because the notebook is the last judgement of Anders's she can still act on. In the way: no money for timber, and a shaft whose digger was publicly wrong once. Flaw: she will not ask twice. Key relationship: Ruth. At the end she has water and has stopped being family to Ruth. Speaks in numbers and short fragments. From S04 on she wears Anders's coat, sleeves rolled twice (new).
- **C02 RUTH**, 60s, head of the council, Anders's sister (concept). Wants the village's one attempt spent where water has always been. She read Anders's number for the council shaft aloud to the council herself (new), and it collapsed (concept). Flaw: she will not be wrong about her brother a second time. Speaks in full sentences with reasons. In S02 she enters Ilse's yard through the kitchen door; in S07 she uses the gate (new).
- **ANDERS**, Ilse's husband, well-digger, died two winters ago (concept). Never seen or heard.
- **VILLAGERS**, a queue of about a dozen with pails; two hired VALLEY MEN at the old well (new). None speaks.

### Locations

- **L01 VILLAGE WELL**: stone ring in the square, in the third year of drought (concept). Its own wooden windlass is old and small (new).
- **L02 ILSE'S YARD**: behind her house, uphill from the square, close enough that hammering carries (new). Contains the shaft mouth, the windlass over it, a kitchen door, a side gate, and Anders's plank tool shed. Inside the shed each tool hangs over its own painted outline (new).
- **L03 THE SHAFT**: square, timber-shored by Anders down to eleven metres, dry (concept). A fixed ladder runs down one wall (new). Water is expected at fourteen metres (concept).

### Props

- **P01 NOTEBOOK**: Anders's, small, cloth-bound. One page reads "14 m" in pencil (concept). Lives in Ilse's apron pocket (S02), then the coat pocket (S06, S07).
- **P02 WINDLASS**: Anders's iron-cranked windlass with its bucket and hauling rope. Stands over the shaft in L02 in every scene and never leaves it (new).
- **P03 KNOTTED LINE**: Anders's measuring line, one knot per metre, fourteen knots, the fourteenth bound in red thread (new). Hangs from the shaft rim. At eleven metres the last three knots lie coiled on the floor; at fourteen the line hangs straight and the red knot touches the floor.
- **P04 SHED PLANKS**: grey, weathered, with the painted tool outlines on their inner faces (new). They become the shoring from eleven to fourteen metres.
- **P05 CASHBOX**: the council's, tin, holding money for one attempt only (concept). Full in S01, empty by the end of S05.
- **P06 ANDERS'S COAT**: hangs on a nail in the shed until S04; Ilse wears it from then on (new).

## Scenes

S01. EXT. VILLAGE WELL - DAY

A bucket knocks on dry stone, far down.

A dozen VILLAGERS stand in line with empty pails. Cracked earth. RUTH cranks the well's small wooden windlass herself. The bucket comes up. She tips it over the rim: a handful of grit.

Ruth sets the tin CASHBOX on the well's stone ring and opens the lid.

                    RUTH
          One dig is all we can pay for. We
          go down here, where the water has
          always been.

The line moves. Coins drop into the tin, one villager at a time.

ILSE reaches the box with a pail in each hand. She looks at the grit on the stones. She walks past the box and out of the square, uphill.

Ruth watches her go, then holds the box out to the next villager.

Turn: the village's money is committed to the old well, and Ilse has publicly kept hers out of it.

S02. EXT. ILSE'S YARD - DAY

Ruth's hand is on the iron crank of ANDERS'S WINDLASS. Behind her, the kitchen door stands open.

                    RUTH
          The council needs the windlass.

Ilse is on her knees at the shaft mouth, dragging the last cover board aside. The KNOTTED LINE hangs from the rim into the dark. She takes the NOTEBOOK from her apron pocket and holds it open toward Ruth.

                    ILSE
          Fourteen.

Ruth does not look at the page.

                    RUTH
          He wrote a number for the council
          shaft too. I read it out to them
          myself.

                    ILSE
          Eleven's dug. Three to go.

                    RUTH
          With what? There is money for one
          hole, Ilse.

Ilse stands and puts her own hand on the crank, beside Ruth's. Neither moves. Ruth lets go first.

                    RUTH (CONT'D)
          Then don't send to us for timber.

Ruth leaves through the kitchen door and pulls it shut behind her.

Turn: Ilse keeps the windlass and loses the council's help; the two women are now on opposite sides.

S03. INT. THE SHAFT - DAY

Lantern light. Ilse stands at the bottom, eleven metres down. Anders's timber shoring ends at her ankles. The last three knots of the knotted line lie coiled on the floor, the red one on top.

She digs. The bucket fills. Below the last timber, the wall is bare earth, a hand deep, then a forearm deep.

A sheet of soil lets go of the bare wall and slides over her boots.

She stands still. More soil trickles. She presses her palm flat against the wall and holds it there until the trickle stops.

She looks up the ladder at the small square of daylight, and begins to climb.

Turn: Ilse learns she cannot go a metre deeper without timber, and she has none.

S04. EXT. ILSE'S YARD - DUSK

Ilse stands in the doorway of Anders's shed. His tools hang on the plank wall, each over its own painted outline. His COAT hangs on a nail.

She takes the tools down and lays them in a row on the ground, the saw first, the mallet beside it. Empty outlines stay behind on the planks.

She takes the coat off its nail and puts it on. She rolls each sleeve twice.

She sets a crowbar under the first plank and leans on it. The nails shriek. The plank comes away, the outline of the saw on its inner face.

Turn: Ilse begins destroying the one place left as Anders kept it, to pay for the dig herself.

S05. EXT. VILLAGE WELL - DAY

A hired iron drill rings on rock, deep in the old well. Two VALLEY MEN work the rope. The bucket comes up and is tipped out: dry chips of stone, a grey heap of them already beside the ring.

Ruth counts the last coins from the cashbox into a valley man's hand. The tin is bare.

From uphill, over the roofs: a hammer on wood, steady.

The villagers in the square turn their heads toward it. Ruth does too. She shuts the empty cashbox and turns back to the well.

Turn: the village's one attempt is spent on dry rock, and Ruth knows Ilse is still digging.

S06. INT. THE SHAFT - NIGHT

Three metres of new shoring line the shaft below Anders's timber: grey shed planks, the painted outline of the saw on one, the mallet on another.

Ilse, in the coat, stands on the floor. The knotted line hangs straight beside her. The red knot touches the ground.

The floor is dust.

She takes out the notebook, looks at the page, puts it back in the coat pocket. She lifts the spade and drives it in beside the red knot.

The sound is wrong: a wet slap.

She pulls the spade out. The cut darkens. Water wells up through it and spreads, reaches the red thread, and soaks it black.

Ilse sits down on the floor, in the water.

Turn: there is water at fourteen metres; Anders's number was right.

S07. EXT. ILSE'S YARD - MORNING

The windlass creaks. Ilse cranks. The bucket comes up full and she pours it into a villager's pail.

The queue runs from the side gate across the yard. It crosses a pale rectangle of bare ground where the shed stood. Anders's tools lie in their row along the house wall. The kitchen door is shut.

Ruth reaches the front with one pail. Ilse fills it. Neither woman looks up from the water.

The knotted line hangs drying on the windlass post, its red thread dark. Ruth looks at it. She looks down the shaft, where the planks begin.

                    RUTH
          That was his shed.

                    ILSE
          It's holding.

Ruth lifts her pail with both hands. She walks back along the queue, across the pale rectangle, and out through the gate.

The next villager sets down a pail. Ilse takes hold of the crank.

Turn: the village has water and Ilse's yard is now the village's; Ruth leaves as a stranger would.

## Known weaknesses

- The ending finds water, so Anders is vindicated. The cost is carried only by the shed and by Ruth using the gate; if that reads as too soft, the alternative within the concept is a dry floor at fourteen.
- S04 to S06 compress several days of lone digging and shoring into two scene breaks; the shed's disappearance is the only clock.
- S03, S04 and S06 have no dialogue. Runtime for those scenes is estimated by beat, not by word count, and should be re-timed at the shot stage.
- The council's failure at the old well (S05) is caused partly by rock, not only by Ilse keeping the windlass.
