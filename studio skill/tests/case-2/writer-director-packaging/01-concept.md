# Concept: The Dry Well

Subject: In the third year of a drought, a hill village's only well runs dry.
Protagonist: ILSE, 60s, widow of the village well-digger.
Angle: Her late husband left a second shaft unfinished behind their house. The village council wants to spend its last money deepening the old well instead. Ilse must decide whether to finish the shaft herself.

Central question: Will she trust her husband's judgement over the village's, and what does it cost her if she does?

Story facts (fiction; all invented, none historical):
- The husband, ANDERS, died two winters ago. His shaft is eleven metres deep and dry.
- His notebook says water lies at fourteen metres. He was wrong once before, publicly; a shaft he dug for the council collapsed.
- The council head, RUTH, is Ilse's sister-in-law (Anders's sister).
- The village has money for one attempt only.

Permitted: invent minor villagers and the details of the dig.
Out of scope: no flashbacks showing Anders alive; no supernatural element; the drought does not end by rain.
Ending constraint: whether or not water is found, Ilse loses something she cannot get back.

Format: long-form, target runtime 3 minutes. No narrator.
Similarity risks: drought and well stories are common; the differentiator is the family split and the notebook.
