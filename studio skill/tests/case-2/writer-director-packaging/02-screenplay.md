# Screenplay: The Dry Well

## Logline

In the third year of a drought, a well-digger's widow tears down her dead husband's workshop to finish the shaft the village voted against, staking his name on the fourteen metres written in his notebook.

## Synopsis

The village's only well comes up dry. Ruth, the council head, puts the last of the money into deepening it and reminds everyone that her brother Anders was sure once before, when his council shaft collapsed. Ilse, his widow, goes home and starts on his unfinished shaft alone, with a goatherd boy at the windlass and the boards of Anders's workshop as shoring. She ties a red rag on the rope at fourteen metres, the depth in his notebook. At fourteen the clay is dry. She sends the boy home and digs on by herself through the night, and two metres further down she strikes water. In the morning the council's dig has ended on rock and the village comes to her yard. Ruth hauls the bucket and sees the rag come up dry, two knots above the wet rope. Ilse unties the rag. The village has its water; the workshop is a bare frame, and Anders was wrong.

## Structure note

A single escalating situation, measured in metres: 11, 12, 13, 14, then past the number. The story has one question (is the notebook right?) and a depth count gives a silent film its clock without a narrator. The turn sits at fourteen, where the answer is no and she has to keep digging without him.

## Character and world facts

The whole story is fiction (profile.md). "Concept" marks a fact fixed in 01-concept.md; "new" marks an element invented in this screenplay under the concept's permission to invent minor villagers and details of the dig.

### Characters

- **C01 ILSE** (concept). 60s, widow of the village well-digger.
  - Wants: to finish Anders's shaft and reach the water at fourteen metres.
  - Why: if the water is there, the collapse stops being the last thing the village knows about him.
  - In the way: the vote, no money, no timber, her age, a shaft nobody has shored below eleven metres.
  - Flaw: she treats the notebook as settled and will not let anyone question it, herself included.
  - Key relationship: Ruth.
  - At the end: she has the water and no longer has the number.
  - Voice: short fragments. Answers a question with an instruction or a different fact.
  - Look (new): grey apron with one deep pocket where the notebook rides.
- **C02 RUTH** (concept). 60s, council head, Anders's sister.
  - Wants: to spend the one attempt where it cannot shame the family again.
  - Voice: complete sentences, counts things, uses names.
  - Look (new): carries the council cash tin in S01; stone dust on her sleeves in S07.
- **C03 JONAS** (new). 12, goatherd. Wants water for his goat. Speaks only in questions and reports of what he sees.
- **C04 ANDERS** (concept). Dead two winters. Never seen. Present only through the notebook, the painted depth marks and the workshop.
- **C05 VILLAGERS** (new). About a dozen, unnamed, no lines.

### Locations

- **L01 VILLAGE SQUARE** (new detail). Stone well-head at the centre, cracked earth, no green anywhere.
- **L02 ILSE'S YARD** (concept: shaft behind the house). The shaft mouth with a timber windlass over it; beside it Anders's plank workshop; a rail fence; the back step of the house.
- **L03 THE SHAFT** (concept: eleven metres, dry). Plank-shored walls. Anders painted a white number at each metre, 1 to 11 (new). Below 11 the wall is bare earth until Ilse shores it. A ladder runs the full depth (new).

### Props

- **P01 NOTEBOOK** (concept). Anders's. The page that matters: a drawing of the shaft, a wavy line across the bottom, "14 m" beside it (drawing is new).
- **P02 ROPE** (new). Windlass rope, one knot per metre. From S03 a red rag is tied at the fourteenth knot.
- **P03 WORKSHOP BOARDS** (new). Weathered planks; become the shoring for metres 12 and below. Ilse chalks 12, 13, 14 on them.
- **P04 OLD WELL BUCKET** (new). Iron-banded, the village's.
- **P05 CASH TIN** (new; concept: money for one attempt only).
- **P06 GOAT** (new). Jonas's, thin, on a rope lead.

## Scenes

S01. EXT. VILLAGE SQUARE - DAY

A rope runs out over the well-head. Far below, the bucket knocks on stone. No splash.

RUTH hauls it up, hand over hand. A dozen VILLAGERS watch the rope. The bucket clears the rim. She tips it. A fist of grey mud drops on the flagstones.

Ruth sets the cash tin on the well-head and opens it. A thin layer of coins.

                    RUTH
          This pays for one dig. We take
          this well down until it's wet.

ILSE steps out of the crowd holding a notebook open at a drawing: a shaft, a wavy line, "14 m".

                    ILSE
          Three metres. Behind my house.

                    RUTH
          He was sure at the council shaft,
          Ilse. I stood next to him.

Hands go up around the square, one after another, for Ruth. Ilse closes the notebook, puts it in her apron pocket and walks out between the raised arms.

Turn: the village's one attempt goes to the old well; Ilse is left with the notebook and no money.

S02. EXT. ILSE'S YARD - DAY

A crowbar bites under a plank. Nails shriek. The board comes off the workshop wall and Ilse lays it on a stack of four.

Through the gap: a workbench, tools hung in a row, an empty coat hook.

JONAS stands at the fence with a thin GOAT on a lead.

                    JONAS
          Is there water in it?

                    ILSE
          At fourteen.

She drags the stack to the shaft mouth and looks down the ladder, then at the windlass handle.

                    JONAS
          Who turns that when you're down
          there?

Ilse puts her foot on the first rung. Jonas ties the goat to the fence.

Turn: Ilse has started taking Anders's workshop apart, and she is no longer digging alone.

S03. INT. THE SHAFT - DAY

A circle of white sky overhead, Jonas's head at its edge. White numbers painted down the planks, ending at 11 just above Ilse's shoulder. Below it, raw earth.

She swings a short pick. Fills the bucket. Tugs the rope twice. The bucket rises.

She wedges a workshop board against the bare wall, hammers it tight and chalks "12" on it.

Up top, Ilse counts knots along the slack rope with her thumb. At the fourteenth she ties a strip of red cloth.

In the shaft again. Another board. She chalks "13". Her hands are wrapped in rags now.

Turn: the shaft is deeper than Anders left it, and fourteen is marked on the rope where both of them can see it.

S04. INT. THE SHAFT / EXT. ILSE'S YARD - DUSK

A board with "14" chalked on it, level with Ilse's knee. Her pick comes down. A dry crack.

She kneels and lifts a lump of grey clay. Closes her fist. It runs out between her fingers as dust.

At the windlass, the red rag sits at the lip of the shaft. Jonas leans over.

                    JONAS
          The rag's at the top. Is it wet?

Ilse takes the notebook from her apron. The wavy line. "14 m". She looks at the floor of the shaft, and at the page.

                    JONAS (CONT'D)
          Ilse?

                    ILSE
          Take your goat home.

Jonas waits. Nothing more comes up. He unties the goat and goes. The windlass handle stands still against an orange sky.

Turn: the notebook is wrong, and Ilse has sent away the only help she had.

S05. INT. THE SHAFT / EXT. ILSE'S YARD - NIGHT

The workshop is a frame of posts against the stars. The workbench top is gone.

Ilse lowers it down the shaft on the rope, climbs down after it, wedges it under the "14" board. She does not chalk a number.

Lantern light. She digs, fills the bucket, climbs eleven-and-more metres of ladder, hauls the bucket up herself, empties it, climbs down. Her breath is loud in the shaft.

Again. The red rag goes down past the lip and out of the lantern's reach.

At the bottom she swings. The pick does not ring. It sinks to the handle and stays.

She pulls it free. The hole it leaves fills, dark and shining. Water spreads around her boots.

Ilse stands in it. Above her head, at the edge of the light, the chalked "14".

Turn: there is water, two metres below where Anders said it would be.

S06. EXT. ILSE'S YARD - DAWN

Ilse asleep sitting against the windlass post, the rope slack in her lap. Beside her a full bucket, the yard dust dark around it.

Jonas at the fence with the goat. The goat pulls the lead out of his hand, walks to the bucket and drinks.

Jonas looks at the goat, the wet rope, Ilse. He runs.

Turn: the village is about to know.

S07. EXT. ILSE'S YARD - MORNING

Villagers line the fence with empty buckets. Ruth stands at the windlass, stone dust grey to her elbows, the old well bucket hooked on Ilse's rope.

She turns the handle. Rope winds on, dry knot after dry knot. The red rag rises out of the shaft, dry. Ruth's hand slows.

One more knot. Another. Then the rope comes up dark and dripping.

Ruth holds the handle still. She looks at the rag, and at Ilse on the back step with the notebook shut on her knee.

                    RUTH
          Fourteen, he said.

                    ILSE
          Fill your bucket, Ruth.

Ruth lifts the bucket clear. Water slops over the iron bands.

                    RUTH
          They will ask me how deep.

Ilse gets up, crosses to the windlass and unpicks the red rag from the rope. She closes her hand on it.

Ruth watches her do it. Then she carries the bucket to the fence, and the first villager comes through the gate.

Ilse sits down on the step. Past her, the line of buckets moves through the yard, between the well and the bare posts of the workshop.

Turn: the village has water; the workshop is gone, the number is gone, and the question between Ilse and Ruth is left unanswered.

## Rejection checklist verdict

No rejections on items 1 to 15. Notes on the closest calls:

- #5, S07, RUTH: "They will ask me how deep." Kept: it is the only line that makes the depth a public matter and forces Ilse's action with the rag.
- #7: one list of three remains, S06, "the goat, the wet rope, Ilse". No others found.
- #13: the water at sixteen metres is not a rescue by coincidence; Ilse reaches it by digging past the mark alone, and it costs her the notebook's claim.
- #15: estimate 3:00 against 3:00. The timing is action-led and unproven until an animatic; S05 is the scene most likely to run long.

## Known weaknesses

- The council's failed dig at the old well is never shown; it is carried only by the stone dust on Ruth's sleeves and the empty buckets in S07. A viewer may miss it.
- "Council shaft" in S01 is the only reference to Anders's earlier collapse. It tells the audience he failed publicly but not that the shaft fell in.
- Jonas has no ending of his own after S06.
